Human-Computer Interaction & Recommendation Lab

The Human-Computer Interaction and Recommendation (HCIR) Lab at the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee's School of Information Studies investigates how people interact with information, search and recommendation systems, and artificial intelligence. Our research brings together information retrieval, human-computer interaction, cognitive psychology, and machine learning to understand and support human information seeking, judgment, and decision-making.

A central focus of our work is understanding how human cognitive limitations, biases, and heuristics interact with the capabilities and limitations of AI systems. We develop behavioral models, evaluation methods, experimental platforms, and interventions to make generative and agentic AI systems more reliable, equitable, and supportive of human judgment.

Our research covers human-centered AI evaluation, conversational and generative information retrieval, cognitive vulnerability and digital resilience, AI-assisted decision-making, and responsible personalization. We combine computational modeling and machine learning with controlled experiments, human evaluation, and participatory research methods.

Founded in 2020, the HCIR Lab is directed by Dr. Jiqun Liu, Associate Professor specializing in AI and Data Science in the School of Information Studies at the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee. The lab was originally established at the University of Oklahoma and transitioned to UWM in 2026.

Join Us

We are actively looking for self-motivated students to join the lab and work on cutting-edge problems at the intersection of people, information, and AI. Research opportunities are available at both undergraduate and graduate levels. We are especially interested in students with backgrounds or interests in any of the following:

  • Human-Computer Interaction and Information Retrieval, including interactive search, conversational AI, and recommender systems;
  • Machine Learning and Natural Language Processing (NLP), including large language model (LLM) evaluation and auditing;
  • Behavioral experiments and user studies, drawing on cognitive psychology, behavioral economics, or quantitative and qualitative methods;
  • Human-centered and responsible AI, including AI literacy, fairness, and the societal impacts of AI.

If you are interested, please email jiqunliu@uwm.edu with your CV and a brief description of your previous research experiences and current research interests.

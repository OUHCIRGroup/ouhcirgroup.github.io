Trustworthy AI Technologies and Experiences (TATE)

Trustworthy AI Technologies and Experiences (TATE) is a research initiative within the HCIR Lab at the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee. TATE investigates how people interact with increasingly capable AI systems, how cognitive limitations and algorithmic behavior may jointly shape human judgment, and how AI technologies can be designed and evaluated to support informed, equitable, and resilient decision-making.

Our research integrates information retrieval, machine learning, human-computer interaction, behavioral science, and participatory methods. We seek to develop reproducible evaluation frameworks, AI auditing methods, and practical interventions that address cognitive vulnerability, unreliable AI assistance, and harmful patterns of personalization.

Research Themes

Cognitive Vulnerability and Human Judgment

Understanding how framing, anchoring, primacy effects, functional fixedness, and other cognitive shortcuts influence human–AI interactions.

AI Auditing, Reliability, and Bias Mitigation

Developing benchmarks, controlled experiments, and auditing methods for identifying unreliable or biased AI judgments and interactions.

Agentic AI, Personalization, and Human Agency

Examining how AI agents learn from interaction histories, retain user preferences, and potentially reinforce narrow choices or behavioral patterns over time.

AI Literacy, Learning, and Community Engagement

Evaluating AI-supported education, library services, and accessible technologies that help individuals and communities develop informed and responsible AI practices.

Research Methods and Infrastructure

TATE combines computational evaluation and behavioral research through experimental AI systems, benchmark datasets, controlled and longitudinal studies, and human-centered auditing. Open research infrastructure, including the ECHO platform, supports reproducibility and interdisciplinary collaboration.

Education and Community Engagement

Proposed and developing activities

TATE also seeks to connect research in computing and AI with socially meaningful applications. Potential activities include interdisciplinary student research, postdoctoral research opportunities, community-engaged technology capstones, public-facing workshops on responsible AI, and student research showcases. We aim to develop collaborations across SOIS, UWM's College of Community Engagement and Professions, and external community and industry partners.

These activities are in development and are not currently funded programs. No postdoctoral positions are being advertised at this time.
